One Rival Publishes 161 Case Studies And Has A Page For Conwy And Gwynedd. You Have Five Pages.

248
URLs published by JM Renewables. You publish 5.

They are not better installers. They have simply written more down.

You hold MCS, NAPIT, RECC and TrustMark — the full set. Neither of the two firms below publishes a stronger accreditation list than yours.

But one of them has a dedicated page for Gwynedd and another for Conwy, and you do not have a page for solar.

The Two Rows That Decide This
They have a page for your town. You do not have a page for your trade.

JM Renewables publishes /area/gwynedd/ and /area/conwy/. When somebody in your county searches for solar panels, Google has a page written specifically about that county to show them — and it is not yours.

This is not a technology gap or a budget gap. It is a writing gap. They sat down and wrote 26 area pages and 161 case studies. Every one of those is permanent and keeps working.

You have almost certainly done more than 161 jobs. You have just never written any of them down.

And here is the row neither of them can ever copy

Neither JM Renewables nor Goodwin Electrical publishes in Welsh. We checked both.

In Gwynedd, Ynys Môn and Conwy that is a genuine and defensible advantage. A Cardiff or Cheshire firm can out-publish you on volume tomorrow. What they cannot do is become a Welsh-speaking local firm.

The answer is not to abandon the Welsh — it is to have both. A Welsh page and an English page for every service and every town. Each ranks separately, and the Welsh ones face almost no competition at all.

Where You Can Win
This is a much weaker field than the page counts suggest
  • Nobody here is retargeting. No Meta pixel found on either competitor. Neither is running the cheapest advertising available to them.
  • Goodwin publishes AggregateRating markup — that is what puts gold stars next to a result. It is a one-off technical job and you could have it too.
  • JM's 161 case studies are their whole moat, and case studies are the easiest thing you own. Six people, eight jobs a week — that is a case study a day if you wanted it, with photographs you are already taking.
  • You hold all four accreditations. Neither rival publishes the complete set. On a £10,000 purchase that is the difference between a quote and a booking.
  • Welsh is uncontested. Zero competition, in your own counties, in your own language.
And where they are genuinely ahead

Both measure and you do not. Both have GA4; you have nothing. They can see which page produced which enquiry and you cannot — so every decision they make is informed and every decision you make is a guess.

JM covers Cheshire, the Wirral and all six North Wales counties. They are not a local firm competing with you; they are a regional firm that has decided your county is worth a page. That should tell you the county is worth more than five pages to you.

What We Could Not Check
Marked honestly rather than guessed
  • Everyone's Google Business Profile, reviews and map position, including yours. On local renewables work a great deal of enquiry starts there and we have no reliable way to measure it.
  • Whether any firm here, including you, currently holds the accreditations displayed — we read websites, not registers.
  • Anyone's revenue, staffing, pricing, install volume or win rate, including yours.
  • Whether any of them advertise on Google or Meta, which is a separate auction we have not assessed.
  • Every renewables installer in North Wales. This is the set we could find and verify, not a census, and it excludes the national installers who buy leads into your area.

They wrote 161 case studies. You did the work and wrote none of them down.

And not one of them can publish in Welsh in Gwynedd. That part is yours alone.
