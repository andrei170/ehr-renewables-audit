You Hold MCS, NAPIT, RECC And TrustMark. Your Website Has Five Pages And Not One Of Them Is About Solar.

0
pages about any service you actually sell

Your accreditations are the strongest we have seen on any renewables firm we have audited. Nobody searching can find them.

Your whole site is five pages: a home page, a contact page, a business details page, a cookie policy and a privacy policy. There is no solar page, no battery page, no heat pump page, no EV charger page and no electrical page.

Site read 22 September 2026
First — What You Have That Most Firms Do Not
In your own words, from your own home page

"Fel contractwyr a gymeradwyir gan NAPIT ac aelodau o TrustMark, MCS a RECC..."

  • MCS — the certification that grant-funded and scheme-backed renewables work generally requires. Without it a customer cannot access most of those routes at all.
  • NAPIT approved contractor — your electrical competence, certified.
  • RECC and TrustMark — the consumer-protection schemes homeowners are told to look for.
  • LocalBusiness structured data and a written meta description — more than most trade sites have.
  • A tappable phone number — 07748 115429.

That is the full set. You are not missing credentials. You are missing every page a customer would use to find them.

Finding 1 — Five Pages, And Three Are Legal Or Contact
Every page you publish: / Home — 381 words /cysylltu Contact /manylion-busnes Business details /polici-cwcis Cookie policy /privacy-policy Privacy policy Pages about what you sell: Solar PV none Battery storage none Heat pumps none EV charging none Electrical work none Grant-funded work none
Google cannot show a page that does not exist

When somebody in Gwynedd or Conwy searches for solar panels, a heat pump, or a battery, Google looks for the best page about that thing. You have not written one, so you are not in the running — regardless of how good the work is or how strong the accreditations are.

You told Ruchi the work comes from word of mouth and Facebook. That is exactly what happens when the website cannot compete: the business grows through people who already know you.

Finding 2 — The Site Is In Welsh. Most Searching Is Not.
Site language: Welsh throughout Page titles: "Tudalen Gartref" (Home Page) URLs: /cysylltu · /manylion-busnes · /polici-cwcis English-language pages: none
This is a real strength and a real gap at the same time

The Welsh is an asset and we would not touch it. In Gwynedd, Ynys Môn and Conwy it signals a local firm in a way no English-language competitor can copy, and Welsh-language searches have almost no competition.

But your own title tag says "Renewables and Electrical Work" in English, so you already know English customers matter. They currently arrive at a site they cannot read, with no pages about what they came for.

The answer is not to replace the Welsh. It is to have both — Welsh pages for Welsh searches, English pages for English ones. Each one ranks separately, and you are the only firm in the area positioned to own both.

Finding 3 — The Accreditations Are Not Searchable
MCS is the one that opens the funded work

Grant and scheme-backed renewables work generally requires the installer to be MCS certified. You are. Homeowners researching that funding are told to look for it, and many search for it by name.

You have no page for any of it — no MCS page, no page explaining what the schemes mean for a homeowner, no page for a customer asking "can I get help paying for this".

Your accreditations appear once, in one Welsh sentence, on one page. They should be a page each, in both languages, with the registration numbers on them.

Finding 4 — Nothing Is Measured
Google Analytics not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found
You are on Facebook with no pixel

You told Ruchi that Facebook is one of your two channels. Without a pixel you cannot advertise to anybody who has looked at your site and not called — the cheapest audience there is — and you cannot tell which Facebook activity produced work.

At an average job value of around £10,000, a single job you cannot trace is worth more than a year of the tools needed to trace it.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Write the service pages. Solar, battery, heat pumps, EV charging, electrical — in Welsh and in English. This is the whole gap and everything else is secondary to it.
  • Give the accreditations their own page, with registration numbers, and a plain page explaining what MCS means for a homeowner looking at funded work.
  • Put the meter on — analytics, a Facebook pixel and call tracking — so the channel you are already using can be measured and retargeted.
  • Build the towns — Caernarfon, Bangor, Llandudno, Conwy, Pwllheli, Porthmadog — in both languages.
  • Then advertise, once there is somewhere for the traffic to land and a way to count it.

Six people, eight jobs a week, and every one of them from somebody who already knew you.

You have the credentials. You have not written the pages that let strangers find them.
